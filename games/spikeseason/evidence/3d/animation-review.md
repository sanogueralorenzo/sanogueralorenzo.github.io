## Repaired blocking findings

### P0 — Facial expression overwrites the mouth's physical scale

The initial `athlete.gd` created the lip sphere with scale `(0.027, 0.007, 0.005)`, but `update_pose()` assigned `mouth.scale.y` to `1.0` or `3.2`. This replaced the seven-millimeter radius with one or 3.2 meters. The close-up clearly showed the resulting narrow vertical spike passing through the torso and above the head. The parent now preserves the original radius and multiplies it by an expression factor; the restarted close-up confirms the repair.

### P0/P1 — Procedural shells appear inside out

The initial close-up showed a hollow-looking jersey with the near-facing surface missing and exposed elbow cuts. `geometry.gd` used the same reversed triangle order for both `profile()` and `lock_mesh()`. Godot's [SurfaceTool documentation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.0/classes/class_surfacetool.html) specifies clockwise front-face winding. The parent reversed triangle order and regenerated normals; the restarted close-up confirms the shirt, face, limbs, and hair now render outward.

## Animation and anatomy concerns requiring focused runtime recheck

- **Planted-foot rotation:** world-space foot positions are retained, but the shoe inherits the root's continuously ball-facing yaw. A planted shoe can therefore swivel in place while the body tracks the ball. Store the planted foot's world orientation as well as its position, and use a step turn when the body needs a large change of direction.
- **Takeoff and landing continuity:** the jump branch resets `feet_world` to each root-relative home every frame. Feet should preserve their takeoff placement/orientation and transition into the jump pose; resetting them under a rotating root risks snaps at takeoff and landing. This is a source-level risk pending sequential-frame confirmation.
- **Handedness and palm intent:** the original procedural thumb used `+side_sign`, placing it on the outside for the intended forward-facing, fingers-up set/block palms. This has been corrected in source to `-side_sign`; the windup close-up now shows thumbs pointing inward. Sets still need cupped, inward-facing wrists rather than two identical upright finger fans.
- **P1 — Spike silhouette, demonstrated:** both arms rise to similar shoulder-relative targets (`athlete.gd:191–194` in this reviewed revision). The actual windup capture confirms that this reads as set/block. The reference's attack has an unmistakable hitting elbow drawn behind the head, shoulder/trunk rotation, and a separate balancing/guiding arm. Give the hitting arm a readable cock-back, overhead extension, and cross-body follow-through while the free arm performs a different action.
- **Contact validation, partial:** `contact_error` is calculated in the actor but is not present in review telemetry. The manual overhead sequence demonstrates the ball meeting the raised striking hand for one ordinary roll. Receive and set contact remain unverified. Receiving should meet a joined forearm platform; setting should cradle above the brow. A score-state transition alone does not prove visible contact. The receive algorithm permits radius 1.05 plus squad/timing/upgrade reach while the visible dive shift is capped at 0.38 and arm reach at 0.615, with horizontal presentation scale 1.1. Long legal digs therefore need special attention; an actual visible miss has not yet been demonstrated.

## Remaining demonstrated mesh issues

- **P1 — Facial integration:** eye white radii `(0.040, 0.022, 0.017)` at `x=±0.061, z=-0.087` protrude substantially past the curved face (`athlete.gd:93–105`). The corrected close-up and upward-looking windup both show googly white spheres rather than integrated anime eyes. Shallow almond-shaped whites, lids, and iris surfaces following the face would improve expression much more than extra small accessories.
- **P1 — Joint continuity:** separate shoulder/upper-arm and elbow/forearm lofts expose hard, abrupt cuts during raised-arm poses (`athlete.gd:65–66, 201–203`). Add local deltoid and elbow transition volumes or overlapping shaped ends so these read as lean connected anatomy.
- **P2 — Garment panels:** boxes at `x=±0.173`, depth 0.22, do not follow the narrower waist (`athlete.gd:44`). In the corrected close-up they visibly stand off the torso. Surface-following panels and restrained seam thickness would read as cloth.
- **P2 — Hair mass:** the radial lock distribution (`athlete.gd:117–126`) produces a uniform crown of spikes. The reference has directional masses, temple shapes, and coherent fringe flow. Author those shapes per identity before increasing lock count.

## Highest-value mesh refinements after the blocking repairs

1. Make the shirt, face, and limb shells render outward and read as continuous volumes. Then add localized shoulder/elbow/knee transitions where uncapped separate lofts expose cuts during bending.
2. Judge facial proportions only after removing the giant mouth and restoring the facial shell. Reduce the exposed eyeball volume and build eyelid/upper-lid shapes integrated into the face; avoid approving the current googly-eye appearance as expressive anime anatomy.
3. Replace evenly scattered hair locks with several authored directional clumps per identity: coherent fringe, temple/side masses, crown flow, and a distinct silhouette. Random radial locks should not be the main identity cue.
4. Shape deltoids, upper-arm taper, calf asymmetry, and the lower ribcage/waist relationship into lean athletic proportions. Use garment seams/folds to support volume, not detached rectangular panels as the primary shirt silhouette.
5. Re-evaluate the bodies from the final rally camera as well as close-up. The reference makes all six players readable while the nearest athletes are large enough to show facial intent, bent knees, and hand shapes. Tiny articulated figures do not satisfy that target.
