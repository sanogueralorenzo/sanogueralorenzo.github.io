# Overall art-direction verdict

Reviewed the final ordinary gameplay images `maps/final-coast-gameplay.png`, `maps/final-gardens-gameplay.png`, and `map-review/final-s6-normal-correction.png` directly against the approved `volleyball-art-direction-3v3.png`. This judgment uses normal framing, not debug closeups or promotional poses. No additional runtime was needed for this explicit image-comparison question.

**Verdict: the original visual-target bar is not fully met.** The current game is a coherent, authored stylized 3D adaptation, and it is clearly beyond a bare primitive blockout. The prior finite construction, portrait, map-placement and readability corrections pass. Nevertheless, the remaining difference is substantial enough that I would treat overall art direction as unresolved work relative to the user's unusually high requested target, rather than certify completion by calling every remaining gap a documented limitation.

The procedural-only constraint limits where assets come from; it does not itself authorize a lower visual standard. Conversely, simple geometry is not automatically unfinished: the real court/net, coherent team palette, original hair/face identities, cream HUD, foliage shadows and distinct venues are all deliberate successful choices. This verdict concerns the combined final image, not the mere presence of primitives in source.

## Why the overall verdict is stricter than the scoped passes

1. **Athletes still do not carry the visual composition.** In the ordinary coast and Harbor frames, the near figures occupy roughly a quarter of image height and the far figures are much smaller; architecture and empty court dominate. Faces are principally communicated by small HUD portraits. The approved image makes athletic silhouettes, directional faces and coordinated action the primary attraction. Some difference is expected between a dramatic illustrated rally and an ordinary serve, but the Gardens rally frame also retains the distant toy-like body read. The earlier camera change was a meaningful improvement, not evidence that this target relationship was fully achieved.
2. **Character material/form treatment remains much simpler than the target.** At ordinary scale the jerseys and skin form broad nearly uninterrupted light surfaces. The target's convincing athletic bodies depend on stronger shoulder/torso/leg plane separation, compression at joints and garment tension/fold shapes. Our inspected frames now avoid broken joints and have purposeful portrait expressions, but those corrections alone do not supply this remaining visual weight. This is not an assessment of temporal animation quality from still images; animation needs its own runtime evidence.
3. **Scenery is inviting but still visibly schematic.** Warm summer colors, trees, seaside buildings, boats, lighthouse and gardens establish place. Broad flat masonry bands, regular rectangular windows/roofs, nearly uniform tree masses, simple distant land shapes and limited visible rocky coastal fallaway remain prominent in the final normal frames. They describe the right setting with less natural layering and surface variation than the painterly coastal target. The reference's appeal is not just its object list or palette.

## Finite priorities for a further art pass

- Increase the **ordinary foreground athlete presence** modestly through court/camera composition while preserving the already-passed corner visibility; assess a serve, receive and coordinated attack, not one staged closeup. Alternatively reduce the visual dominance/contrast of the largest background masses. The goal is an athlete-led image, not an arbitrary zoom amount.
- Give the **existing character meshes** stronger authored cloth and anatomical planes: a few broad jersey tension folds at shoulders/waist, short-fold shapes at hips, clearer knee/elbow compression and controlled warm/cool shading. Judge these at native gameplay and portrait size. More tiny pores or higher polygon count would not address the problem.
- Add **large-scale coastal/environment layering** visible from the authored camera: meaningful rocky fallaway beside the coast court, broken facade/setback depth and less regular canopy/silhouette groupings. Preserve a quiet background behind the ball. More scattered small props would have little effect.

These are substantive visual-authoring priorities rather than newly discovered functional defects. They should not invalidate the demonstrated work already done, and they do not require external assets. If the project is packaged now, the accurate description is “a coherent stylized procedural 3D adaptation with substantial reference-fidelity limits.” That description is honest, but it is not equivalent to saying the user's complete art-direction requirement has been demonstrated.

No opinion about fun, championship completion, fairness, saving, performance or the full game's readiness is inferred from this art comparison. No reviewer runtime remains running.


## Prioritized finite next pass: normal-frame targets

Coordinates below refer to the 1280×720 final gameplay images. Sizes are approximate visual measurements, not automated image metrics. The reference is proportionally normalized to 720 pixels high for comparison; its dramatic jump pose is not used as the only size benchmark.

1. **Foreground athlete presence, first.** Coast Ren is approximately 180 pixels tall, Jun approximately 155; Harbor's near figures are around 175. The reference's near cover player is approximately 215 pixels at this scale, and its setter approximately 265. Try a measured 15–25% increase in near athlete screen presence. The backed-out coast net spans approximately X=305–985 (53% of width), compared with approximately 61% in the reference. Roughly 10–15% tighter authored framing is a reasonable starting point, while keeping the net's top around Y=300 and explicitly retaining the passed left-serve/deep-right contact visibility. This is a composition adjustment, not a request to double body size or change athletic physics. Check an ordinary receive and coordinated attack as well as serve before deciding it worked.
2. **Broad cloth/anatomical planes, second.** Near Ren's back jersey around X=213–258/Y=512–562 and Jun's front around X=874–910/Y=463–514 are nearly flat. Add two or three broad authored shoulder/waist tension folds, a controlled shorts fold at hips, and soft warm/cool separation on the existing upper-arm/thigh/calf masses. At normal scale these should read as approximately 2–4 pixel form changes, not noisy microtexture. Keep the clear number panel and all fixed closed armhole junctions. Assess the native frame before spending time on additional small mesh detail.
3. **Visible rocky coastal fallaway, third.** Coast X=0–280/Y≈290–500 is largely water/plain walkway. The analogous target area has irregular rocks and vegetation descending to foam. Recompose the existing slope/rock geometry so three to five layered angular rock masses and their waterline are actually visible in this normal-camera region beyond the rail. Merely adding more geometry hidden below the platform cannot improve the frame. Preserve ball contrast and keep all rock/tree volume outside the gameplay court.
4. **Break and quiet broad backdrop masses, after the first composition check.** Coast facade area X≈520–980/Y≈70–315 and Gardens retaining bands X≈20–1220/Y≈140–330 carry large flat visual weight. Use stronger but coherent recess/awning/balcony shadow planes and a few uneven planted or setback breaks, rather than many extra small props. Background treatment should support the now-larger athlete silhouettes. Existing visible map identities and corrected lighthouse support should remain intact.

The priority is 1+2, then 3. Reassess the integrated ordinary image before extending item 4; do not mechanically add all suggested detail without checking its visual effect. The goal is a finite improvement to character-led composition and authored form, not an endless demand for literal pixel equivalence to an illustration.
