# Cozy Sora runtime verification

Verified on 4 September 2026 with Godot 4.7.2, Forward+, Metal, Apple M3 Max. No automated tests were created. Verification used native play, rendered captures, source review, and engine logs.

## Original destination integration

The game starts on Cozy Sora destination selection without generating the map. That integration originally registered Seabreeze Village alone; Harbor Hills is covered below. Its preview is a capture of the playable procedural coast. Final layouts were inspected at 320 × 640, 390 × 844, 844 × 390, 1280 × 720, and 1920 × 1080. Narrow and short layouts retain accessible Settings and Play actions; larger layouts use a landscape preview beside the map description. Short screens omit secondary copy where needed, and content can scroll.

Native interaction covered keyboard selection and focus outlines, mouse buttons, settings focus cycling, volume and mute changes, restoring settings, map loading, pause/resume, cat/seagull selection, returning, and repeated re-entry. An independent reviewer completed two full entry/return cycles; the primary review completed additional cycles. Each playing session reported one player, one camera, and one audio player. Every observed return reported zero players, cameras, audio players, and orphan nodes. Settings were restored to 100% sound, unmuted, with manual touch controls disabled.

Touch controls were exercised through their native mouse interaction path: movement pad, right-side look, mode switch, pause, and return. The final seeded map was also played after the foliage correction: the cat walked clear of the starting foliage, and the seagull climbed and flew toward the coast. Real touch events ignore emulated mouse duplicates. The 320-pixel layout keeps the pad and action hit areas separate. Controller bindings and menu focus paths were inspected in code.

## Preserving Seabreeze Village

Before/after runtime captures covered the normal cat camera and ten scenic views: coast, paddy, farm, rail, village, alley, vending, viaduct, shrine, and top. An independent critic initially rejected the comparison because foliage placements changed when the cache regenerated.

Fresh runs of the original code also produced different foliage. Two helper methods in the custom seeded generator called bare `randf()`, which Godot resolves to its global random function. Qualifying those two calls as `self.randf()` fixes the preexisting nondeterminism. This is the only intentional change to the map's generation algorithm; authored terrain, roads, landmarks, buildings, props, palette, atmosphere, and placement rules remain unchanged.

The original application and the refactored application were then freshly rendered with that identical two-line correction. Both generated 833 trees and shrubs. The independent critic inspected all eleven paired views and confirmed matching foliage placements, canopy shapes, roads, fields, architecture, props, camera composition, materials, and fog. Remaining frame differences were consistent with shader wind, water/cloud timing, and particles. Older randomly generated caches can have different canopies; the corrected seed now gives a repeatable world. The selector preview was refreshed from this final coast.

## Performance and review

The immediate pre-refactor warm capture loaded in 13.1 seconds during concurrent workstation load. Refactored native review entries took 13.3–14.4 seconds under contention and 6.1 seconds in the final run. Fresh corrected generation took 14.3 seconds in the original application and 15.6 seconds in the refactored application, including its transition and frame yields. The final native gameplay usually held 120 FPS with roughly 0.4–0.7 ms physics processing. Capture and heavily contended runs varied substantially; these observations are runtime checks, not controlled benchmarks.

Independent reviews approved the landing design, ownership boundaries, repeated scene lifecycle, and final paired village views after fixing focus handling, settings isolation, invalid scene handling, narrow layouts, and the seeded-generator bug. Final runtime and import logs contained no script errors or crashes.

## Platform limits

Physical controller hardware, genuine simultaneous multi-finger input, and audible output were not independently verified. Native input handling, settings, synthesized audio lifecycle, pause, and teardown were exercised or inspected. The responsive UI was rendered at mobile and desktop sizes; no browser or mobile export was built. Forward+ remains the intended gameplay renderer and does not support Godot's web export. A browser release would require a separate renderer/export compatibility pass. Captured-mouse look remains difficult to assess through native UI automation; its original camera math is retained, and drag-look was exercised with touch controls enabled.

All bundled previews, shaders, meshes, textures, and sounds are project-owned or generated by the project. No external game assets, fonts, models, animations, audio, or map data were added.

## Harbor Hills integration

The second destination registers `harbor_hills` with a native `cable_car` preview, original description, normal café-side cat spawn, dedicated scene and separate generated cache. Its static build contains 252 cypress and 238,455 grass clumps in 78 spatial batches. All new content is procedural and map-owned. No automated tests or external assets were added.

Repeated independent visual review covered normal spawn, waterfront, commercial frontage, residences, park, cable car, stairs, courtyard, roofs, flight and top view. Corrections included terrain-draped street edges, connected sidewalk crossings, level furnished courts, readable shop signs, richer displays and cable-car detail, leaf-card foliage, occupied roof/rear elevations, grounded distant scenery, and stair geometry that shares its landing elevation. The final eleven-view Harbor set received independent visual acceptance, including a final review of corrected ramp joins and foundations that reach the lowest terrain corner. The independent gameplay critic also gave final acceptance after rechecking every identified traversal blocker.

All eleven Seabreeze views were rendered before and after this integration. The independent critic accepted preservation of terrain, road layout, buildings and props, foliage placement, character/camera framing, palette and atmosphere. Remaining pixel differences were consistent with moving wind, water and shadows. No Seabreeze generator or shader changed. Its warm capture entry took 6,111 ms, compared with 6,244 ms for the pre-integration capture on this workstation.

Native walking, jumping, flight and ground perching were exercised through keyboard and touch-control pointer input. The first gameplay review caught floating cross-street ribbons; those now sample terrain at both edges. Follow-up play exposed the small curb and stair entrance joins that scenic captures alone did not establish; dedicated lowered crossing approaches, a stair lead-in and removal of the overlapping sidewalk resolve those interfaces. The independent final stair check walked from Z = 45 through 62, 78 and 91, then back down through 79, 61 and 45 to 30, remaining grounded without jumping. The pavilion ramp was also traversed to its roof, and cat-to-gull switching preserved that elevated position. A focused roof check landed the gull at Y = 55.568 m and returned the cat to the same roof at Y = 55.398 m. The workshop's rising ramp and flat bridge were walked continuously onto its roof after aligning their join. Under a solid shop canopy, upward gull motion stopped below the awning and switching returned the cat to the forecourt. At the bay, the cat stopped at Z = −116.486 m; the gull settled at the water surface (Y = −0.78 m), and switching recovered the last safe promenade position. New Harbor-only support queries and capsule clearance checks preserve roof height, guard overhead switching and recover unsafe sea positions. Seabreeze continues using its existing controller branch.

The final independent Harbor → selector → Seabreeze → selector → Harbor → selector sequence passed. Harbor warm re-entry took 840 ms and restored its normal spawn from its own cache. The sequence reported one player, one camera, two audio players in Harbor and one audio player in Seabreeze. Every return reported zero active sessions, players, cameras, audio players and orphan nodes. The release run observed a complete 160-second cable-car round trip: Z = −60.33 at 20 seconds, −16.95 at 40, 43.05 at 60, the hilltop at 96 with 180° heading at 80, then 66.80, 30.95 and −29 on the return, and the waterfront at −100 with its original heading at 160. Bell triggers continued throughout, and pause stopped the simulation clock. The spatial cable-car bell belongs to the map and pauses with its simulation; the shared ambient stream also pauses. Profiling now records viewport size, character position/mode/grounding and bus output level, making native traversal and pause observations inspectable. A paused sample read −200 dB; active Harbor ambience typically read around −30 to −35 dB.

The final cold Harbor capture build took 4,874 ms. Warm focused entry took 744 ms. After spatial grass batching, the corrected stair run reported 112–145 FPS at 1280 × 720 with physics usually around 0.6–0.8 ms, with occasional higher samples. A later complete transit/lifecycle run held 60 FPS in both destinations; its Harbor cold entry took 6,663 ms and Seabreeze warm entry 11,491 ms under the contemporaneous workstation load. Earlier unbatched and contended walkthroughs varied more widely; enlarged Retina windows also increased render cost substantially in both maps. These are observed native runs on one workstation, not controlled performance benchmarks.

The two-card selector was rendered at 1280 × 720, 390 × 844 and 844 × 390, with the welcome header and Settings visible on entry, accessible Play controls, and scrolling/focus navigation to Harbor. A layout-settling scroll fix prevents initial focus from hiding the header; it respects visibility, loading state and a changed focus owner.

Final evidence was captured under `/tmp/harbor-hills/release`, with paired Seabreeze captures under `/tmp/harbor-hills/seabreeze-before` and `/tmp/harbor-hills/seabreeze-after`. The project bundles only its selector preview; the README capture commands reproduce the inspection views. Controller hardware and physical multi-touch remain the platform limits described above.

## Harbor Hills quality pass · 5 September 2026

Harbor Hills was compared with Seabreeze Village's normal cat view, inhabited village and coast, and with its own eleven original viewpoints. Five substantive visual iterations addressed sparse street edges, disc-like foliage, bare park turf, unfinished terrain transitions, shop forecourts, garden seating and simple boats. Two independent critics accepted the fifth iteration at the first map's demonstrated visual standard. A subsequent final review accepted the 110 m grass visibility limit and confirmed preservation of Seabreeze's start, coast and village captures. Their acceptance concerns visuals; native runtime verification was performed separately.

The pass adds generated leaf-spray textures, broader tree crowns, curved grass with the shared grass shader, painted surface variation, steep-cut stone shading, planted curb beds, bicycles, stoop details, shop displays, communal garden tables, a furnished waterfront, shaped hulls and rigging, wind-driven laundry, and sparse GPU garden seeds. All content is code-authored or generated. No external assets or automated tests were added. Harbor's normal spawn and map title behavior are preserved.

Native keyboard play covered walking and sprinting from the café, curb jumping, terrace access, the complete stair street in both directions, cat/seagull switching, climbing, flight, ground settling, pause and return to destinations. Close traversal found a reproducible capsule contact trap beside a café wall: input was present, but all six slide contacts repeated the same sloped-ground normal without displacement. A Harbor-only 0.015 m recovery margin resolved this. Re-entry at the same position could back away, and the cat subsequently walked from the sidewalk over the café ramp to its forecourt. Stair traversal went from Z = 45 through 53.72 to 97.58 and back down to 26.25 without jumping, remaining grounded. Flight climbed above the neighborhood and settled in the park. Paused character positions remained fixed and audio read −200 dB. Return to selection reported zero active sessions, players, cameras, audio players and orphan nodes. The cable car completed its existing 160-second circuit during the run.

Temporary spawn changes and contact logging used for the focused native walkthrough were removed. The sole shared controller change is the recovery margin for maps opting into surface traversal; Seabreeze does not use that branch. No Seabreeze generator, shader or map data changed. Both destinations were rendered again after the final code changes. Engine import and final runtime logs contained no script errors or crashes.

Final cold generation took 7,463 ms and the final warm entry took 778 ms on the same M3 Max/Metal setup. Native play commonly reported 50–120 FPS at 1280 × 720, with physics generally around 0.5–1.0 ms. During computer-use capture and workstation contention, observed rates varied down to single digits; enlarged Retina windows also changed rendering cost. These are observations from interactive verification, not a controlled performance benchmark. Physical controller and multi-touch limitations remain as recorded above.

Evidence for this pass is under `/tmp/harbor-polish`: `before`, `reference`, five review rounds, `final`, `reference-after`, and native play logs. The bundled Harbor preview is a new native 1280 × 720 cable-car view. The README commands reproduce the scenic captures.
