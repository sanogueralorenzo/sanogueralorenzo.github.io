## World and movement constants

The coast centerline is `z = -12.5 - 0.0022 x² + 0.00001 x³`; road half-width is 4m. The inland loop reaches approximately ±57m east/west and z68m north. The shrine hill is centered at (0,30), with a 27m radius, 8m plateau radius and 8m height contribution. Paddies sit at elevation 0.4m, farm ground at 2.6m, and village ground at 3m. The elevated railway is at 11.5m and crosses the gully between x12 and x44.

The normal camera has a 62° vertical field of view. The cat begins at (-6.4, 0.2), with yaw −0.08 and pitch 0.09; camera follow distance is 4.2m. Cat walking/sprinting speeds are 3.6/6.4m/s, gravity 22m/s², and jump impulse 6.2m/s. The gull has 9.5/17m/s flight/boost targets, automatic gliding and ground perching, and a 4.5m flight follow distance.

## Godot implementation choices

The port uses Godot meshes, shaders, collision bodies, particles, audio generation, and UI rather than adapting the site's JavaScript or running a browser. Source textures are regenerated by original image routines, including their material tint multiplication. GPU vegetation is grouped into spatial MultiMeshes. Static architectural primitives are merged without moving their collision bodies. Terrain and foliage caches contain only this project's generated output.

Godot's Forward+ color management and shadow pipeline differ from Three.js. Lighting and grading therefore require rendered-view comparison; copying raw light intensities alone produces incorrect exposure. The Godot painterly pass uses overlapping low-variance brush regions instead of the source's tensor-based anisotropic filter. Both retain silhouettes while softening fine procedural detail.
